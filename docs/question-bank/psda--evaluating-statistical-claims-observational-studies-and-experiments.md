### 1. psda-bicycle-survey-generalize

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A surveyor selected a random sample of 120 residents of an apartment complex and asked each whether they own a bicycle. Of those sampled, 45 own a bicycle. To which of the following populations can the result of the survey most appropriately be generalized?

- **A.** All residents of the apartment complex
- **B.** All residents of the city where the apartment complex is located
- **C.** All residents of the state where the apartment complex is located
- **D.** All residents of the apartment complex who own a bicycle

**Answer:** A

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** A result from a random sample can be generalized only to the population from which the sample was selected. The sample was selected at random from the residents of the apartment complex, so the estimate (45/120 = 37.5%) applies to all residents of the apartment complex. Distractors: the city and the state were not sampled, so residents who live outside the complex may differ from those inside it; the residents who own a bicycle are a subgroup of the complex, but the sample was drawn from all residents, not from bicycle owners, so restricting the conclusion to owners misstates what was estimated.

</details>

### 2. psda-claims-online-poll-self-selected

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A town's website invited visitors to answer an online poll about a new recycling rule. Of the 900 visitors who chose to respond, 72% opposed the rule. Why is this result unlikely to represent the opinions of all town residents?

- **A.** The poll asked about only one rule.
- **B.** The percent who opposed the rule is greater than 50%.
- **C.** The number of respondents, 900, is too small to describe a town.
- **D.** The respondents selected themselves, and residents with strong opinions are more likely to respond.

**Answer:** D

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** A result represents a population when the sample is chosen at random from it. Here the visitors chose whether to respond, which is a voluntary response sample: residents who feel strongly, often those opposed, are more likely to answer, and residents who do not visit the website are not reached at all. Distractors: 'only one rule' describes the topic of the poll, not how the sample was chosen; 'greater than 50%' is a fact about the result, not a reason it is biased; 'too small to describe a town' is wrong because 900 responses would be plenty for a random sample; the problem is how the respondents were selected, not how many there were.

</details>

### 3. psda-sampling-method-walk

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A principal wants to estimate the proportion of all students at a school who walk to school. Which of the following sampling methods is most likely to produce a sample that is representative of all students at the school?

- **A.** Selecting 50 students at random from those who arrive at school by bus
- **B.** Selecting 50 students at random from a list of all students at the school
- **C.** Selecting the first 50 students who arrive at the school one morning
- **D.** Selecting 50 students who volunteer to answer questions about walking to school

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** A sample represents a population when every member of the population has the same chance of being selected, which a random selection from a list of all students provides. Distractors: students who arrive by bus are, by definition, not walkers, so that sample excludes the group being studied; the first 50 students to arrive one morning are chosen by arrival time, which is related to how they travel; students who volunteer to answer questions about walking are self-selected and are likely to differ from students who do not volunteer.

</details>

### 4. psda-claims-fitness-center-exercise-bias

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

To estimate the mean number of hours per week that a town's adults exercise, a researcher surveyed 150 adults as they left a fitness center in the town. Which statement best describes the estimate from this sample?

- **A.** It is likely to be too high, because adults at a fitness center tend to exercise more than other adults.
- **B.** It is likely to be too low, because adults leaving a fitness center have finished exercising for the day.
- **C.** It is likely to be accurate, because 150 adults is a large sample.
- **D.** It is likely to be accurate, because every adult surveyed lives in the town.

**Answer:** A

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Adults leaving a fitness center are a convenience sample, and the way they were chosen is related to the quantity being measured: people who go to a fitness center tend to exercise more than adults who do not. The sample mean is therefore likely to overestimate the mean for all the town's adults. Distractors: 'too low ... finished exercising for the day' confuses one visit with the weekly total the survey asks about; '150 adults is a large sample' is wrong because a larger biased sample is still biased; 'every adult surveyed lives in the town' is true but irrelevant, since the sample still overrepresents frequent exercisers.

</details>

### 5. psda-claims-hens-supplement-extend

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A farmer randomly selected 60 of the 900 hens on a farm and randomly assigned half of them to receive a feed supplement. Over a month, the hens given the supplement laid more eggs on average. To which hens can the conclusion that the supplement increases egg production be appropriately extended?

- **A.** Only the 30 hens that received the supplement
- **B.** Only the 60 hens in the study
- **C.** All 900 hens on the farm
- **D.** All hens on every farm

**Answer:** C

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Random assignment of the 60 hens to the two groups supports the causal conclusion that the supplement increases egg production. Random selection of those 60 hens from the 900 hens on the farm allows the conclusion to be extended to the population sampled, all 900 hens on the farm. Distractors: 'only the 30 hens that received the supplement' ignores the comparison group, which is what makes a causal conclusion possible; 'only the 60 hens in the study' ignores the random selection from the farm; 'all hens on every farm' extends the conclusion beyond the one farm that was sampled.

</details>

### 6. psda-claims-muscle-cream-placebo

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

In an experiment, adults with sore muscles were randomly assigned to two groups. One group received a cream containing a new ingredient, and the other received an identical-looking cream without it. What is the main purpose of giving the second group the identical-looking cream?

- **A.** To separate the effect of the ingredient from the effect of simply applying a cream
- **B.** To allow the results to be generalized to everyone with sore muscles
- **C.** To make the two groups the same size
- **D.** To make sure that the ingredient works for every adult in the study

**Answer:** A

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The second group receives a placebo: a cream that looks the same but lacks the ingredient. Both groups then go through the same experience (applying a cream and expecting relief), so any difference in soreness between the groups can be attributed to the ingredient itself. Distractors: 'generalized to everyone with sore muscles' depends on how the adults were selected, and nothing says they were a random sample; 'the same size' is a matter of how many adults go in each group, not of what the control group receives; 'works for every adult in the study' confuses a comparison of group averages with a guarantee for each person.

</details>

### 7. psda-claims-plant-food-random-assignment

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A researcher randomly assigned 80 tomato plants to receive either a new plant food or plain water. After six weeks, the plants given the plant food were taller on average. What is the main reason for assigning the plants to the two groups at random?

- **A.** To make the groups alike in other ways, so that the difference in height can be attributed to the plant food
- **B.** To allow the results to be generalized to all tomato plants
- **C.** To guarantee that every plant given the plant food grows taller
- **D.** To increase the number of plants in the study

**Answer:** A

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Random assignment spreads other differences among the plants (sunlight, soil, starting size) evenly between the two groups, on average. Then the only systematic difference between the groups is the plant food, so a difference in average height can be attributed to it. That is why random assignment supports a causal conclusion. Distractors: 'generalized to all tomato plants' describes what random selection from a population does, not random assignment, and these 80 plants were not selected at random from all tomato plants; 'every plant given the plant food grows taller' confuses an average effect with a guarantee for each plant; 'number of plants in the study' is fixed at 80 whether the assignment is random or not.

</details>

### 8. psda-claims-practice-app-self-chosen

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A teacher let each student in a class decide whether to use a practice app before a test. Students who used the app scored higher on average than students who did not. Why does this study not show that using the app caused the higher scores?

- **A.** Students who chose to use the app may differ in other ways, such as motivation, from those who did not.
- **B.** Some students in the class did not use the app.
- **C.** The scores were compared using averages instead of individual scores.
- **D.** The app was used before only one test.

**Answer:** A

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The students were not randomly assigned to use the app; they chose. Students who choose extra practice may be more motivated or better prepared, and those differences, not the app, could explain the higher average. Without random assignment the study shows only an association. Distractors: 'some students in the class did not use the app' is what provides a comparison group, so it is needed, not a flaw; 'compared using averages' is the correct way to compare two groups; 'before only one test' limits how widely the result applies but is not the reason a causal claim fails.

</details>

### 9. psda-claims-walking-blood-pressure

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

Researchers surveyed a random sample of 500 adults in a county. Adults who reported walking at least 30 minutes a day had lower average blood pressure than those who did not. Which conclusion is most appropriate?

- **A.** Daily walking causes lower blood pressure among adults in the county.
- **B.** Daily walking causes lower blood pressure among all adults.
- **C.** Daily walking is associated with lower blood pressure among adults in the county, but it may not cause it.
- **D.** Daily walking is associated with lower blood pressure among all adults, but it may not cause it.

**Answer:** C

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Two features of the design decide the conclusion. The adults were randomly selected from the county, so the result can be generalized to adults in the county, but not beyond. The adults were not randomly assigned to walk or not; they reported their own habits, so walkers may differ from non-walkers in other ways (diet, age, health), and the study shows only an association. Distractors: 'causes lower blood pressure among adults in the county' claims cause from an observational study; 'causes lower blood pressure among all adults' claims cause and also extends the result beyond the county; 'associated with lower blood pressure among all adults' extends the result to a population that was not sampled.

</details>

### 10. psda-fitness-stretching-experiment

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A researcher randomly selected 200 members of a fitness center and randomly assigned each to one of two groups. One group followed a stretching routine; the other did not. After 8 weeks, the stretching group was more flexible on average. Which of the following is the most appropriate conclusion?

- **A.** The routine likely caused the greater flexibility, and the result can be generalized to all members of the fitness center.
- **B.** The routine likely caused the greater flexibility, and the result can be generalized to all adults.
- **C.** The routine is associated with greater flexibility but may not have caused it, and the result can be generalized to all members of the fitness center.
- **D.** The routine is associated with greater flexibility but may not have caused it, and the result can be generalized to all adults.

**Answer:** A

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Two features of the design decide the conclusion. The members were randomly assigned to the two groups, so the only systematic difference between the groups is the routine, and the difference in flexibility can be attributed to it: a causal conclusion is appropriate. The members were randomly selected from the fitness center, so the result can be generalized to all members of the fitness center, but not beyond, because no one outside the center was sampled. Distractors: 'all adults' extends the result to a population that was not sampled; 'may not have caused it' ignores the random assignment, which is what supports a causal conclusion.

</details>

### 11. psda-claims-green-tea-design-change

*Hard · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A study recorded whether each of 300 randomly selected employees of a company drinks green tea and the number of sick days each took in a year. Tea drinkers averaged fewer sick days. Which change to the study would best support a conclusion that drinking green tea reduces sick days?

- **A.** Selecting 600 employees at random instead of 300
- **B.** Selecting the employees at random from several companies instead of one
- **C.** Asking each employee how many cups of green tea they drink each week
- **D.** Randomly assigning the employees to drink green tea or not, then recording their sick days

**Answer:** D

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** A conclusion that one thing causes another needs random assignment to the treatment. In the original study the employees chose whether to drink green tea, so tea drinkers may differ from others in other ways (exercise, diet, age) that also affect sick days. Randomly assigning the employees to drink green tea or not balances those differences between the groups, so a difference in sick days can be attributed to the tea. Distractors: 'selecting 600 employees' makes the estimate more precise but the study is still observational; 'several companies instead of one' widens the population the result can be generalized to, which is a question of random selection, not of cause; 'how many cups of green tea' measures the habit in more detail but the employees still choose their own habit.

</details>

### 12. psda-claims-screen-time-website

*Hard · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A website asked its visitors to report their daily screen time and nightly hours of sleep. Of the 2,000 visitors who responded, those who reported more screen time tended to report less sleep. Which conclusion is most appropriate?

- **A.** More screen time causes less sleep, and the result applies to all people.
- **B.** More screen time causes less sleep, but the result applies only to people like those who responded.
- **C.** More screen time is associated with less sleep among all people, because 2,000 is a large sample.
- **D.** More screen time is associated with less sleep among those who responded, but no causal or general conclusion is supported.

**Answer:** D

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The study has neither random assignment nor random selection. The visitors chose their own screen time, so the study can show only an association, not a cause; and the visitors chose whether to respond, so the result describes only those who responded and cannot be generalized. Distractors: 'causes less sleep, and the result applies to all people' fails on both counts; 'causes less sleep, but the result applies only to people like those who responded' limits the population correctly but still claims a cause without random assignment; 'because 2,000 is a large sample' is the trap: a large self-selected sample is still not representative, so the size does not justify generalizing to all people.

</details>

### 13. psda-claims-weekday-morning-shoppers

*Hard · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A grocery store selected a random sample of 200 customers from those who shopped there on weekday mornings during one month. Of these, 62% said they use the store's mobile app. To which group can this result most appropriately be generalized?

- **A.** Only the 200 customers in the sample
- **B.** All customers who shopped at the store on weekday mornings during that month
- **C.** All customers who shopped at the store during that month
- **D.** All grocery shoppers in the region

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** A result from a random sample can be generalized to the population the sample was selected from, and no further. The customers were selected at random from those who shopped on weekday mornings during that month, so that is the population. Distractors: 'only the 200 customers' ignores that a random sample does support a generalization to its population; 'all customers who shopped at the store during that month' includes evening and weekend shoppers, who had no chance of being selected and may use the app at a different rate; 'all grocery shoppers in the region' goes even further beyond the population that was sampled.

</details>

### 14. psda-lavender-volunteers-generalize

*Hard · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A researcher recruited 80 volunteers at a university and randomly assigned each to sleep with or without a lavender scent in the room. After two weeks, those with the scent slept 20 minutes longer per night on average. Which of the following is the most appropriate conclusion?

- **A.** The scent likely caused the longer sleep, and the result can be generalized to all students at the university.
- **B.** The scent likely caused the longer sleep, but the result cannot be generalized beyond the volunteers in the study.
- **C.** The scent may not have caused the longer sleep, but the result can be generalized to all students at the university.
- **D.** The scent may not have caused the longer sleep, and the result cannot be generalized beyond the volunteers in the study.

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Random assignment of the volunteers to the two conditions means the only systematic difference between the groups is the scent, so the difference in sleep can be attributed to the scent: a causal conclusion is appropriate. The participants were volunteers, not a random sample of any larger population, so the result cannot be generalized beyond the volunteers in the study. Distractors: 'all students at the university' generalizes from a volunteer sample that was not randomly selected; 'may not have caused' ignores the random assignment, which is what makes the causal conclusion valid; 'may not have caused ... but can be generalized to all students at the university' makes both errors; 'may not have caused ... and cannot be generalized' keeps the correct limit on generalization but still denies the causal conclusion that random assignment supports.

</details>
