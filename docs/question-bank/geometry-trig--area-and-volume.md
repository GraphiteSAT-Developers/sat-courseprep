### 1. geo-cone-cylinder-equal-volume-height

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A right circular cone and a right circular cylinder have bases with equal radii, and the two solids have equal volumes. The height of the cylinder is 5 units. How tall, in units, is the cone?

- **A.** $\frac{5}{3}$
- **B.** $5$
- **C.** $10$
- **D.** $15$

**Answer:** D

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The volume of the cylinder is πr^2(5) = 5πr^2, and the volume of the cone is (1/3)πr^2h. Setting the volumes equal yields (1/3)πr^2h = 5πr^2, and multiplying both sides by 3/(πr^2) yields h = 15. Therefore, the cone is 15 units tall. Distractors: 5/3 divides the cylinder's height by 3 instead of multiplying; 5 assumes that equal volumes need equal heights; 10 uses a factor of 1/2 for the cone, as in the area of a triangle, instead of 1/3.

</details>

### 2. geo-cone-radius-tripled-volume

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A right circular cone has a volume of 40 cubic units. A second right circular cone has the same height as the first cone, and its radius is 3 times the radius of the first cone. What is the volume, in cubic units, of the second cone?

- **A.** $120$
- **B.** $240$
- **C.** $360$
- **D.** $1,080$

**Answer:** C

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** It's given that the second cone has the same height as the first cone and 3 times its radius. The volume of a cone is (1/3)πr^2h, so multiplying the radius by 3 multiplies r^2 by 3^2 = 9 while the height factor is unchanged. Therefore, the volume of the second cone is 9(40) = 360 cubic units. Distractors: 120 multiplies the volume by 3, treating volume as proportional to the radius; 240 multiplies by 2(3) = 6, doubling the factor instead of squaring it; 1,080 multiplies by 3^3 = 27, as if the height had been tripled as well.

</details>

### 3. geo-parallelogram-slanted-side-area

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A parallelogram has a side of length 15 units and an adjacent side of length 9 units. The height of the parallelogram, measured perpendicular to the 15-unit side, is 8 units. The area $A$ of a parallelogram is given by $A=bh$, where $b$ is the length of a base and $h$ is the height to that base. What is the area of the parallelogram, in square units?

- **A.** $48$
- **B.** $60$
- **C.** $72$
- **D.** $120$

**Answer:** D

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The area of a parallelogram is its base times its height, where the height is measured perpendicular to the base. It's given that the height perpendicular to the 15-unit side is 8 units, so the area is 15 · 8 = 120. The 9-unit side is slanted, so it is not the height. Therefore, the area of the parallelogram is 120 square units. Distractors: 48 is the perimeter, 2(15 + 9); 60 is (1/2)(15)(8), the triangle formula; 72 is 9 · 8, which uses the slanted side in place of the base.

</details>

### 4. geo-prism-to-cube-edge

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: either · techniques: T9, T11*

A right rectangular prism has dimensions 4 inches by 6 inches by 9 inches. A cube has the same volume as the prism. What is the edge length, in inches, of the cube?

- **A.** $4$
- **B.** $6$
- **C.** $9$
- **D.** $36$

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** It's given that the prism measures 4 by 6 by 9 inches, so its volume is 4 · 6 · 9 = 216 cubic inches. A cube with edge length e has volume e^3, so e^3 = 216 and e = 6. Therefore, the edge length of the cube is 6 inches. Distractors: 4 and 9 are the shortest and longest dimensions of the prism, not the edge of the cube; 36 is the area of one face of the cube (6^2), not its edge length.

The line shows 6. Multiplying 4 · 6 · 9 = 216 and recognizing 6^3 by hand is just as quick.

### 5. geo-rectangle-perimeter-to-area

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: either · techniques: T2*

The length of a rectangle is 3 times its width, and the perimeter of the rectangle is 56 units. What is the area of the rectangle, in square units?

- **A.** $28$
- **B.** $49$
- **C.** $147$
- **D.** $441$

**Answer:** C

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Let w be the width, in units. It's given that the length is 3w and the perimeter is 56, so 2(w) + 2(3w) = 8w = 56 and w = 7. The length is 3 · 7 = 21, so the area is 7 · 21 = 147 square units. Therefore, the area is 147. Distractors: 28 is half the perimeter, the sum w + 3w, reported as the area; 49 is 7^2, the square of the width; 441 is 21^2, the square of the length.

**Desmos** (not yet verified in Desmos). Type:<br><code>2x+2y=56</code><br><code>y=3x</code>

Click the intersection: (7, 21). The width is 7 and the length is 21, so the area is 7 · 21 = 147.

</details>

### 6. geo-rectangular-pyramid-volume

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A right pyramid has a rectangular base that measures 5 units by 8 units, and the height of the pyramid is 9 units. What is the volume, in cubic units, of the pyramid?

- **A.** $40$
- **B.** $120$
- **C.** $180$
- **D.** $360$

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The volume of a pyramid is (1/3)Bh, where B is the area of the base. The base is a 5-by-8 rectangle, so B = 40. Substituting yields (1/3)(40)(9) = 120. Therefore, the volume of the pyramid is 120 cubic units. Distractors: 40 is the base area alone; 180 is (1/2)(40)(9), which uses 1/2 in place of 1/3; 360 is (40)(9), the volume of a prism with the same base and height.

</details>

### 7. geo-sphere-volume-from-diameter

**Answer:** C

Distractors: 12π is (4/3)π(3)^2, which squares the radius instead of cubing it; 27π is π(3)^3, which leaves out the factor 4/3; 288π uses the diameter 6 as the radius, (4/3)π(216).

</details>

### 8. geo-trapezoid-parallel-sides-area

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A trapezoid has parallel sides with lengths 7 units and 13 units, and the perpendicular distance between the parallel sides is 6 units. The area $A$ of a trapezoid is given by $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$, where $a$ and $b$ are the lengths of the parallel sides and $h$ is the distance between them. What is the area, in square units, of the trapezoid?

- **A.** $26$
- **B.** $60$
- **C.** $120$
- **D.** $546$

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The area of a trapezoid is half the sum of the lengths of the parallel sides times the distance between them. It's given that the parallel sides have lengths 7 and 13 and the distance between them is 6, so the area is (1/2)(7 + 13)(6) = (1/2)(20)(6) = 60. Therefore, the area of the trapezoid is 60 square units. Distractors: 26 adds the three given lengths, 7 + 13 + 6; 120 is (7 + 13)(6), which leaves out the factor 1/2; 546 multiplies all three lengths, 7 · 13 · 6.

</details>

### 11. geo-cone-cup-to-cylinder-height

The water filling the cup is poured into an empty cylindrical container with a radius of 3 centimeters. To the nearest tenth, what is the height, in centimeters, of the water in the container?

- **A.** $16.0$
- **B.** $5.3$
- **C.** $3.0$
- **D.** $1.8$

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

Distractors: 16.0 omits the 1/3 in the cone formula, 144/9; 3.0 uses the cone's radius 4 for the cylinder, 48/16; 1.8 divides by 3 a second time, 16/9.

### 12. geo-cone-half-radius-triple-height

The radius of a right circular cone is multiplied by $\frac{1}{2}$, and its height is multiplied by 3, to form a new cone. The volume of the new cone is $k$ times the volume of the original cone, where $k$ is a constant. What is the value of $k$?

- **A.** $\frac{3}{4}$
- **B.** $\frac{3}{2}$
- **C.** $3$
- **D.** $12$

**Answer:** A

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

The new volume is 3/4 times the original volume. Therefore, k = 3/4. Distractors: 3/2 multiplies by 1/2 for the radius without squaring it; 3 ignores the change in the radius; 12 uses 2 in place of 1/2 for the radius factor, (2)^2(3).

</details>

- **C.** $60\pi$
- **D.** $63\pi$

**Answer:** C

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The total surface area of a cylinder is the area of the two circular bases plus the lateral area. Each base has area π(3)^2 = 9π, so the two bases have area 18π. Therefore, the total surface area is 60π square units. Distractors: 42π is the lateral area alone; 51π includes only one base, 9π + 42π; 63π is the volume, π(3)^2(7), not the surface area.

**Desmos** (not yet verified in Desmos). Type:<br><code>2\pi\cdot3^2+2\pi\cdot3\cdot7</code><br><code>60\pi</code>

Both lines show 188.495..., so the choice 60π matches. Desmos is slower than the hand route, because the surface-area setup still has to be done by hand; use it to check the arithmetic, typing each choice until one matches the first line.

**Answer:** 80

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The volume of a cylinder is πr^2h. Increasing the radius by 50% replaces r with 1.5r, and decreasing the height by 20% replaces h with 0.8h.
