### 1. geo-angle-ratio-largest-angle

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

The measures of the three angles of a triangle are in the ratio $2:3:7$. What is the measure, in degrees, of the triangle's largest angle?

- **A.** $15$
- **B.** $30$
- **C.** $45$
- **D.** $105$

**Answer:** D

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The angle measures of a triangle add to 180°. It's given that the measures are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 7, so they are 2a°, 3a° and 7a° for some a, and 2a + 3a + 7a = 180. It follows that 12a = 180, so a = 15. The largest angle measures 7(15) = 105°. Therefore, the answer is 105. Distractors: 15 is the value of one part, a; 30 is the smallest angle, 2a; 45 is the middle angle, 3a.

</details>

### 2. geo-complementary-expressions-x

*Easy · Student-produced response · route: hand-primary · techniques: T1*

Angles $PQR$ and $RQS$ are complementary. The measure of angle $PQR$ is $(3x + 7)^\circ$, and the measure of angle $RQS$ is $(2x - 2)^\circ$. What is the value of $x$?

**Answer:** 17

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Complementary angles have measures that add to 90°. It's given that the measures are (3x + 7)° and (2x - 2)°, so (3x + 7) + (2x - 2) = 90, or 5x + 5 = 90. Subtracting 5 from both sides yields 5x = 85, so x = 17. Check: the angles measure 58° and 32°, and 58 + 32 = 90. Therefore, x = 17. Using 180 in place of 90 (treating the angles as supplementary) is the common slip and gives x = 35.

**Desmos** (not yet verified in Desmos). Type:<br><code>y=(3x+7)+(2x-2)</code><br><code>y=90</code>

Click the intersection, (17, 90), and read x = 17. Solving 5x + 5 = 90 by hand is faster; use the graph as a check.

</details>

### 3. geo-corresponding-angles-solve-x

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: T1*

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. Two corresponding angles have measures $(5x - 14)^\circ$ and $(3x + 22)^\circ$. What is the value of $x$?

- **A.** $4$
- **B.** $18$
- **C.** $21.5$
- **D.** $76$

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** When parallel lines are cut by a transversal, corresponding angles are congruent. It's given that the corresponding angles measure (5x - 14)° and (3x + 22)°, so 5x - 14 = 3x + 22. Subtracting 3x from both sides and adding 14 to both sides yields 2x = 36, so x = 18. Therefore, the value of x is 18. Distractors: 4 comes from a sign error, 2x = 22 - 14 = 8; 21.5 comes from treating the angles as supplementary, 8x + 8 = 180; 76 is the measure of each angle, 5(18) - 14, not the value of x.

**Desmos** (not yet verified in Desmos). Type:<br><code>y=5x-14</code><br><code>y=3x+22</code>

Click the intersection, (18, 76), and read the x-coordinate, 18 (the y-coordinate is the angle measure, not x). The one-step equation is faster by hand; use the graph as a check.

</details>

### 4. geo-equilateral-side-expressions-x

*Easy · Student-produced response · route: hand-primary · techniques: T1*

Triangle $PQR$ is equilateral, with $PQ = 2x + 5$ and $QR = 4x - 9$. What is the value of $x$?

**Answer:** 7

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** All three sides of an equilateral triangle have the same length. It's given that PQ = 2x + 5 and QR = 4x - 9, so 2x + 5 = 4x - 9. Subtracting 2x from both sides and adding 9 to both sides yields 14 = 2x, so x = 7. Check: each side is 2(7) + 5 = 19 and 4(7) - 9 = 19. Therefore, x = 7. Entering the side length, 19, instead of x is the common slip.

### 5. geo-isosceles-vertex-to-base-angle

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

In triangle $PQR$, $PQ = PR$ and the measure of angle $P$ is 44°. What is the measure, in degrees, of angle $Q$?

- **A.** $44$
- **B.** $68$
- **C.** $92$
- **D.** $136$

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** It's given that PQ = PR, so triangle PQR is isosceles and the angles opposite those two sides, angle R and angle Q, have equal measures. The angle measures of a triangle sum to 180°, so 44 + 2m = 180, where m is the measure of angle Q. Subtracting 44 from both sides and dividing by 2 yields m = 68. Therefore, the measure of angle Q is 68°. Distractors: 44 assumes angle Q equals the vertex angle P rather than angle R; 136 is 180 - 44, the combined measure of the two base angles, never halved; 92 is 180 - 44 - 44, treating P and R as the equal pair.

</details>

### 6. geo-linear-pair-ratio-four-to-one

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

Point $B$ lies on segment $AC$ between $A$ and $C$, and point $D$ is not on line $AC$. The measure of angle $ABD$ is 4 times the measure of angle $DBC$. What is the measure, in degrees, of angle $ABD$?

- **A.** $36$
- **B.** $45$
- **C.** $72$
- **D.** $144$

**Answer:** D

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Since B lies on segment AC between A and C, angles ABD and DBC form a linear pair, so their measures add to 180°. Let the measure of angle DBC be a°; then angle ABD measures 4a°, and a + 4a = 180, so 5a = 180 and a = 36. It follows that angle ABD measures 4(36) = 144°. Therefore, the answer is 144. Distractors: 36 is the measure of angle DBC, the smaller angle; 45 is 180/4, which divides by 4 instead of by the 5 equal parts; 72 is 4(90/5), which treats the two angles as complementary.

</details>

### 7. geo-parallel-obtuse-angle

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

Lines $j$ and $k$ are parallel, and a third line intersects both of them. One of the acute angles formed at the intersection of line $j$ and the third line measures 54°. What is the measure, in degrees, of each obtuse angle formed at the intersection of line $k$ and the third line?

- **A.** $36$
- **B.** $54$
- **C.** $126$
- **D.** $144$

**Answer:** C

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** When parallel lines are cut by a transversal, corresponding angles are congruent, so the angles at line k have the same measures as the angles at line j: 54° and 180° - 54° = 126°. The obtuse angles are the ones that measure more than 90°. Therefore, each obtuse angle at line k measures 126°. Distractors: 54 is the measure of the acute angles at line k, not the obtuse ones; 36 is the complement of 54°, but adjacent angles along a line are supplementary, not complementary; 144 is 180° - 36°, built on that same complement error.

</details>

### 8. geo-vertical-angles-expression-measure

*Easy · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: T1*

Lines $AB$ and $CD$ intersect at point $E$. The measure of angle $AEC$ is $(4x + 18)^\circ$, and the measure of angle $BED$ is $(6x - 12)^\circ$. What is the measure, in degrees, of angle $AEC$?

- **A.** $78$
- **B.** $87.6$
- **C.** $102$
- **D.** $156$

**Answer:** A

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Since lines AB and CD intersect at E, angles AEC and BED are vertical angles, and vertical angles are congruent. It's given that their measures are (4x + 18)° and (6x - 12)°, so 4x + 18 = 6x - 12. Subtracting 4x from both sides and adding 12 to both sides yields 30 = 2x, so x = 15. Substituting 15 for x in 4x + 18 yields 78. Therefore, angle AEC measures 78°. Distractors: 87.6 comes from treating the angles as supplementary, 10x + 6 = 180, so x = 17.4 and 4(17.4) + 18 = 87.6; 102 is 180 - 78, the measure of the adjacent angle AED; 156 is 78 + 78, the sum of the two vertical angles.

### 9. geo-alternate-interior-obtuse-angle

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: T1*

A transversal crosses two parallel lines. One pair of alternate interior angles measures $(7x - 30)^\circ$ and $(4x + 15)^\circ$. Of the eight angles formed, what is the measure, in degrees, of any one that is obtuse?

- **A.** $15$
- **B.** $75$
- **C.** $105$
- **D.** $165$

**Answer:** C

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** When parallel lines are cut by a transversal, alternate interior angles are congruent. It's given that they measure (7x - 30)° and (4x + 15)°, so 7x - 30 = 4x + 15, which yields 3x = 45 and x = 15. Each of these angles measures 4(15) + 15 = 75°, so they are acute. At each intersection, each obtuse angle forms a linear pair with a 75° angle, so it measures 180 - 75 = 105°. Therefore, the answer is 105. Distractors: 15 is the value of x; 75 is the acute angle, the intermediate result; 165 is 180 - 15, the supplement of x rather than of the angle.

### 10. geo-dilation-angle-unchanged

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

Triangle $LMN$ is the image of triangle $XYZ$ after a dilation with scale factor $3$, where $L$, $M$, and $N$ correspond to $X$, $Y$, and $Z$, respectively. Angle $X$ measures $38^\circ$ and angle $Y$ measures $64^\circ$. What is the measure, in degrees, of angle $N$?

- **A.** $78$
- **B.** $102$
- **C.** $142$
- **D.** $234$

**Answer:** A

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** A dilation multiplies every length by the scale factor but leaves every angle measure unchanged, so angle N has the same measure as its corresponding angle, angle Z. By the triangle angle sum, angle Z measures 180 - 38 - 64 = 78°. Therefore, angle N measures 78°. Distractors: 102 is 38 + 64, the sum of the two given angles rather than the third angle; 142 is 180 - 38, which subtracts only angle X; 234 is 3(78), which wrongly applies the scale factor to an angle.

</details>

### 11. geo-exterior-angle-x

*Medium · Student-produced response · route: hand-primary · techniques: T1*

In triangle $ABC$, the measure of angle $A$ is $2x^\circ$ and the measure of angle $B$ is $(3x - 12)^\circ$. The exterior angle of the triangle at vertex $C$ has a measure of $(6x - 20)^\circ$. What is the value of $x$?

**Answer:** 8

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles, so 6x - 20 = 2x + (3x - 12) = 5x - 12. Subtracting 5x from both sides and adding 20 to both sides yields x = 8. Check: the angles at A and B are 16° and 12°, the exterior angle at C is 28°, so the interior angle at C is 152°, and 16 + 12 + 152 = 180. Therefore, x = 8. Setting the exterior angle equal to just one interior angle, such as 6x - 20 = 3x - 12, is the common slip and gives x = 8/3.

**Desmos** (not yet verified in Desmos). Type:<br><code>y=2x+(3x-12)</code><br><code>y=6x-20</code>

After writing the exterior-angle relation by hand, the intersection (8, 28) confirms x = 8. The one-step linear equation is faster by hand than typing it.

</details>

### 12. geo-isosceles-exterior-base-angle

*Medium · Student-produced response · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

In triangle $ABC$, $AB = AC$. Side $CA$ is extended beyond $A$ to point $D$, and the measure of angle $BAD$ is $112^\circ$. What is the measure, in degrees, of angle $ABC$?

**Answer:** 56

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Angle BAD is an exterior angle of triangle ABC at vertex A, so its measure equals the sum of the two remote interior angles, angle ABC and angle ACB. It's given that AB = AC, so the angles opposite those sides, angle ACB and angle ABC, are congruent. It follows that 2(angle ABC) = 112, so angle ABC measures 56°. Check: angle BAC is 180 - 112 = 68°, and 68 + 56 + 56 = 180. Therefore, the answer is 56. Entering 34 (half of 180 - 112) uses the interior angle at A as if it were the sum of the base angles.

</details>

### 13. geo-pentagon-flower-bed-fifth-angle

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: either · techniques: T9*

A flower bed in a city park is shaped like a convex pentagon. Four of its interior angles measure $100^\circ$, $115^\circ$, $108^\circ$, and $120^\circ$. What is the measure, in degrees, of the fifth interior angle?

- **A.** $83$
- **B.** $97$
- **C.** $108$
- **D.** $277$

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** The interior angles of a convex polygon with n sides add to 180(n - 2) degrees, so a pentagon's angles add to 180(3) = 540°. The four given angles add to 100 + 115 + 108 + 120 = 443°, so the fifth angle measures 540 - 443 = 97°. Therefore, the answer is 97. Distractors: 83 is 180 - 97, the exterior angle at that vertex; 108 is 540/5, the angle of a regular pentagon, which this flower bed is not; 277 is 720 - 443, which uses the angle sum of a hexagon.

**Desmos** (not yet verified in Desmos). Type:<br><code>180(5-2)-(100+115+108+120)</code>

The line shows 97, the fifth angle in degrees. The arithmetic is just as quick by hand.

</details>

### 14. geo-pond-width-crossing-segments

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: either · techniques: T9*

To find the width $DE$ of a pond, a surveyor places stakes so that segments $AE$ and $BD$ cross at point $C$ and segment $AB$ is parallel to segment $DE$. $AB$ is 18 meters, $AC$ is 12 meters, and $CE$ is 30 meters. What is $DE$, in meters?

- **A.** $7.2$
- **B.** $36$
- **C.** $45$
- **D.** $540$

**Answer:** C

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Since AB is parallel to DE, angle BAC is congruent to angle DEC (alternate interior angles), and angles ACB and ECD are vertical angles, so triangle ABC is similar to triangle EDC, with A matching E and B matching D. Corresponding sides are proportional: DE/AB = CE/AC. Substituting yields DE/18 = 30/12, so DE = 18(30)/12 = 45. Therefore, the pond is 45 meters wide. Distractors: 7.2 is 18(12/30), which inverts the ratio; 36 is 18 + (30 - 12), which adds the difference instead of scaling; 540 is 18(30), which leaves out the division by 12.

**Desmos** (not yet verified in Desmos). Type:<br><code>18\cdot\frac{30}{12}</code>

The line shows 45, the width DE in meters. The single ratio is just as fast by hand.

</details>

### 16. geo-regular-polygon-sides-156

*Medium · Student-produced response · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

Each interior angle of a regular polygon has a measure of $156^\circ$. How many sides does the polygon have?

**Answer:** 15

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Each exterior angle of the polygon forms a linear pair with an interior angle, so each exterior angle measures 180 - 156 = 24°. Alternate approach: setting 180(n - 2)/n = 156 yields 180n - 360 = 156n, so 24n = 360 and n = 15. Therefore, the polygon has 15 sides. Entering 24, the exterior angle measure, is the common slip.

### 17. geo-side-splitter-parallel-length

Points $D$ and $E$ lie on sides $AB$ and $AC$ of triangle $ABC$, respectively, and segment $DE$ is parallel to segment $BC$. If $AD = 4$, $DB = 6$, and $DE = 5$, what is the length of $BC$?

- **A.** $7.5$
- **B.** $9$
- **C.** $11$
- **D.** $12.5$

**Answer:** D

**By hand.** Since DE is parallel to BC, angle ADE is congruent to angle ABC (corresponding angles) and angle A is shared, so triangle ADE is similar to triangle ABC by angle-angle similarity. Corresponding sides are proportional: DE/BC = AD/AB. It's given that AD = 4 and DB = 6, so AB = 4 + 6 = 10. Substituting yields 5/BC = 4/10, so BC = 5(10)/4 = 12.5. Therefore, BC = 12.5. Distractors: 7.5 is 5(6/4), which uses DB in place of AB; 9 is DE + AD, an addition in place of the ratio; 11 is DE + DB, also an addition.

**Desmos** (not yet verified in Desmos). Type:<br><code>5\cdot\frac{4+6}{4}</code>

The line shows 12.5, the length of BC. The ratio is just as quick by hand once AB = 10 is found.

### 18. geo-similar-triangle-perimeter

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: either · techniques: T9*

Triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $DEF$, where vertices $A$, $B$, and $C$ correspond to vertices $D$, $E$, and $F$, respectively. The side lengths of triangle $ABC$ are $AB = 9$, $BC = 12$, and $AC = 15$. If $DE = 6$, what is the perimeter of triangle $DEF$?

- **A.** $16$
- **B.** $24$
- **C.** $33$
- **D.** $54$

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional, so the scale factor from triangle ABC to triangle DEF is DE/AB = 6/9 = 2/3. Perimeter is a length, so it scales by the same factor: the perimeter of ABC is 9 + 12 + 15 = 36, and the perimeter of DEF is (2/3)(36) = 24. Therefore, the perimeter of triangle DEF is 24. Distractors: 16 scales the perimeter by (2/3)^2, the area factor; 33 subtracts the difference 9 - 6 = 3 from the perimeter instead of scaling; 54 uses the inverted scale factor 9/6 = 3/2.

**Desmos** (not yet verified in Desmos). Type:<br><code>k=\frac{6}{9}</code><br><code>k(9+12+15)</code>

k shows 0.667 and the second line shows 24. Taking 2/3 of 36 by hand is just as fast.

</details>

### 20. geo-triangle-inequality-integer-count

*Medium · Student-produced response · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

Two sides of a triangle have lengths $7$ and $12$. The length of the third side is an integer. How many different lengths are possible for the third side?

**Answer:** 13

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** By the triangle inequality, each side of a triangle is shorter than the sum of the other two, so the third side s satisfies s < 7 + 12 = 19 and 12 < 7 + s, which means s > 5. The integers with 5 < s < 19 are 6 through 18, and 18 - 6 + 1 = 13. Therefore, 13 lengths are possible. Entering 14 counts 5 or 19 as allowed (a degenerate, flat triangle); entering 12 subtracts the endpoints, 18 - 6, without adding 1.

</details>

### 21. geo-welded-tubes-third-side

*Medium · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

A welder joins three straight steel tubes end to end to form a triangle. Two of the tubes are 16 inches and 27 inches long. What could be the length, in inches, of the third tube?

- **A.** $10$
- **B.** $11$
- **C.** $40$
- **D.** $43$

**Answer:** C

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** By the triangle inequality, the third side s must be shorter than the sum of the other two and longer than their difference: 27 - 16 < s < 27 + 16, or 11 < s < 43. Of the choices, only 40 lies strictly between 11 and 43. Therefore, the third tube could be 40 inches long. Distractors: 10 checks only s < 43 and misses that 10 + 16 = 26 is less than 27; 11 equals the difference 27 - 16, which gives a flat (degenerate) figure rather than a triangle; 43 equals the sum 16 + 27, again a degenerate figure.

</details>

### 23. geo-congruence-sufficiency

*Hard · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

In triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$, $AB = DE$ and the measure of angle $B$ equals the measure of angle $E$. Which of the following additional pieces of information, taken individually, would be sufficient to prove that triangle $ABC$ is congruent to triangle $DEF$? I. $BC = EF$ II. $AC = DF$ III. The measure of angle $C$ equals the measure of angle $F$.

- **A.** I only
- **B.** I and III only
- **C.** II and III only
- **D.** I, II, and III

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Angle B is at vertex B, the endpoint of side AB. I: BC = EF gives two sides (AB, BC) with the included angle B, so SAS proves congruence. III: angle C = angle F gives two angles (B, C) and a side (AB) not between them, so AAS proves congruence. II: AC = DF gives two sides (AB, AC) with a non-included angle (B), which is SSA; that does not determine a triangle, so it is not sufficient. The answer is I and III only. Distractors: 'I only' misses that AAS is a valid criterion; 'II and III only' accepts SSA and rejects SAS; 'I, II, and III' accepts SSA.

</details>

### 24. geo-equilateral-extension-angle-bad

*Hard · Multiple choice · route: hand-primary · techniques: —*

Triangle $ABC$ is equilateral. Side $BC$ is extended beyond $C$ to point $D$ so that $CD = CA$. What is the measure, in degrees, of angle $BAD$?

- **A.** $120$
- **B.** $90$
- **C.** $60$
- **D.** $30$

**Answer:** B

<details><summary>Solutions</summary>

**By hand.** Each angle of an equilateral triangle measures 60°, so angle BAC and angle ACB are 60°. Angle ACD forms a linear pair with angle ACB, so it measures 180 - 60 = 120°. It's given that CD = CA, so triangle ACD is isosceles and its base angles CAD and CDA are equal: each is (180 - 120)/2 = 30°. Angle BAD is angle BAC plus angle CAD, so it measures 60 + 30 = 90°. Therefore, the answer is 90. Distractors: 120 is angle ACD, an intermediate result; 60 is angle BAC alone; 30 is angle CAD alone.

</details>

- **A.** $8$
