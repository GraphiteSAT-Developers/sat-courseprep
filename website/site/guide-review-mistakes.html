How to review SAT mistakes so they don't come back

Most students check the right answer, nod, and move on. Then they miss the same kind of question on test day. Points come from the review, not from the practice set. Here's a loop that works.

On this page

  1. Which questions to review
  2. Tag the cause
  3. Redo it cold
  4. Bring it back later
  5. Look for patterns

Which questions to review

Not just the wrong ones. Review every question you:

  • got wrong,
  • got right by guessing, or weren't sure about,
  • took much longer than about a minute and a half on (Math) or a minute and ten seconds (Reading and Writing),
  • changed your answer on.

A lucky guess is a miss you haven't paid for yet.

Tag the cause

Before you read the explanation, write down why you think you missed it. Use one of four causes:

  • Content: you didn't know the rule, formula or idea.
  • Reading: you misread the question, answered a different one, or missed a word like "not" or "except."
  • Execution: you knew how, but made an arithmetic, sign or entry slip.
  • Time: you could have got it, but rushed or ran out.

The cause tells you the fix. Content needs a lesson. Reading needs a habit, like rereading the last line. Execution needs a check step. Time needs pacing practice, not more content.

Redo it cold

Read the explanation, close it, and solve the question again from a blank page. If you can't, you haven't learned it yet; read the explanation again. For Math, do it both ways when you can, with Desmos and by hand, so you have a backup route.

Bring it back later

Getting a question right right after reading its explanation proves very little. Getting it right days later proves you learned it. So:

  • Put every reviewed question in a list.
  • Try it again about three days later, cold.
  • Take it off the list only after you've got it right twice on separate days. A miss puts it back at the start.

This is how Graphite's review queue works: anything you miss comes back a few days later until you get it right twice.

Look for patterns

Once a week, count your tags by skill. Three misses in the same skill from the same cause is a pattern, and patterns are where the points are. Put that skill at the top of next week's practice.
