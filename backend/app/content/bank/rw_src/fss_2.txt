## fss medium
P: The fictional scientist Rina Sato and her research assistants ______ frog eggs in local ponds every month for three years.
Q: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A: counts
*A: counted
A: has counted
A: is counting
X: The compound subject "Rina Sato and her research assistants" is plural, and "for three years" describes a completed past period, so "counted" is correct. "Counts," "has counted," and "is counting" are singular.

## fss medium
P: The ______ nests, built from mud and saliva, are attached to the sides of cliffs high above the sea.
Q: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A: swallow's
A: swallows
*A: swallows'
A: swallows's
X: The nests belong to more than one swallow (the verb "are" and the plural "nests" show several), so the plural possessive "swallows'" is correct. "Swallow's" refers to a single bird, "swallows" isn't possessive, and "swallows's" isn't a standard form.

## fss medium
P: The fictional author Marcus Hale dedicated his memoir to his grandmother, whose stories about the family's history ______ him to become a writer.
Q: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A: inspires
A: has inspired
*A: had inspired
A: inspiring
X: The subject of the clause is "stories," which is plural, and the inspiring happened before he wrote and dedicated the memoir, so the past perfect "had inspired" is correct. "Inspires" and "has inspired" are singular, and "inspiring" isn't a finite verb.

## fss hard
P: Of the three proposals the city council reviewed, the plan to convert the old warehouse into apartments, which would add 200 units of housing, ______ the most support from residents.
Q: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A: were receiving
A: have received
*A: received
A: receiving
X: The subject is "plan," which is singular; the phrases "to convert the old warehouse into apartments" and "which would add 200 units of housing" describe it. The past-tense "received" works with a singular subject and matches "reviewed." "Were receiving" and "have received" are plural, and "receiving" isn't a finite verb.

## fss hard
P: The fictional naturalist Ruth Okoye observed that a pair of herons returned to the same nest each spring, and she noted that ______ always arrived within a few days of each other.
Q: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A: it
A: its
*A: they
A: them
X: The pronoun refers to the two herons and is the subject of "arrived," so the plural subject pronoun "they" is correct. "It" is singular, "its" is possessive, and "them" is an object pronoun.

Q: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A: the fictional artist Lina Zhao uses
A: the fictional artist Lina Zhao, who now uses
*A: the fictional artist Lina Zhao's recent paintings use

The other choices compare the paintings with Zhao herself, which is illogical, and the choice with "who now uses" also leaves the sentence without a main verb.

## bnd hard
P: The fictional ecologist Hana Kim tracked lizards on two ______ one, with many predators, and another, with almost none.
Q: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A: islands;
*A: islands:
A: islands,
A: islands
X: An independent clause ending with "two islands" is followed by a phrase that identifies the islands, so a colon is correct. A semicolon requires an independent clause after it. A comma makes the sentence confusing because the phrase that follows already contains commas. No punctuation runs the explanation onto the clause.

## bnd hard
P: The fictional poet Ellen Marsh wrote most of her nature poems after a drought forced her family to sell their ______ critics who once called the poems cheerful now read them as expressions of grief.
Q: Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?
A: farm, many
A: farm many
A: farm, and, many
*A: farm; many
X: The text contains two independent clauses, so a semicolon is needed between them. A comma alone creates a comma splice, no punctuation creates a run-on, and the comma after "and" is incorrect.

## tr medium
P: Scientists once thought that the giant squid lived only in the deepest parts of the ocean and would never be filmed alive. ______ in 2004, Japanese researchers photographed a live giant squid in its natural habitat for the first time.
Q: Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?
*A: However,
A: Similarly,
A: For example,
A: Therefore,
X: The photographs contradict the belief that a live giant squid would never be filmed, so "However" fits. "Similarly" signals likeness, "For example" signals an illustration, and "Therefore" signals a result.
